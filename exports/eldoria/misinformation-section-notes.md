The earlier exposure values retain the original 2021 and 2026 endpoints, with a modest 2023 dip and slower growth in 2025. ## Suggested section copy

> Reported exposure to mis- and disinformation has risen unevenly in Eldoria, with a marked increase at the 2027 briefing. In the fictional exercise figures, 52% of adults report encountering suspected false or misleading information at least weekly, up from 41% in 2026 and 29% in 2021. Concern about online mis- and disinformation reaches 58%, compared with 48% in 2026. These indicators describe the wider information environment; they do not imply that the material is believed or that current outbreak-related misinformation is widespread.
>
> Eldoria has three major national news outlets, and about 70% of adults are active on social media. As an additional exercise assumption, 7% rely exclusively on social media for news, while only 12% of adults say they trust news encountered there. This creates a communication challenge: use of a channel does not necessarily mean confidence in it. The state broadcaster remains the most trusted source overall, but its lower standing among adults under 35 makes it important to repeat clear, attributable updates through channels younger audiences already use.

Keep the existing dated statement that current outbreak-related misinformation volumes remain low, separately from the long-term graph. The package already establishes the three national outlets, 70% social-media activity and weaker broadcaster trust among adults under 35. The 7%, 12% and all annual graph values are proposed fictional additions.

## How to use the images

1. **Channel reliance versus trust.** The annotated second and third paragraphs in image 2 provide the strongest addition. Add a short callout such as “7% use social media as their only news source; 12% trust news on social media.” Define both as shares of all adults; do not imply that 12% is a share of the 7% group. These should be labelled exercise assumptions.
2. **Younger audiences.** Connect social-media reliance to the package's existing under-35 trust gap. Suggest cross-channel communication and visible attribution. The images do not establish that younger people believe more misinformation.
3. **AI news use.** Mention increasing use of AI chatbots alongside low trust as an optional qualitative exercise assumption. No numerical chatbot trend is available in these images, so do not add a measured line for it.
4. **News interest.** A separate, secondary graph could show declining interest in news. Image 2 reports Dutch interest falling from 61% in 2018 to 45% in 2026; those endpoints cannot be relabelled as Eldoria measurements. If adapted, construct and label a separate fictional series rather than mixing it into the exposure graph.
5. **Trust does not have an evidenced downward trend here.** The handwritten “both decreasing” suggestion goes beyond the visible article, which describes overall news trust as still relatively high. Keep trust as a contextual statement unless you deliberately add a fictional decline. Do not equate trust in the state broadcaster, social-media news and AI tools.
6. **Confidence versus concern.** Image 1 describes people feeling able to recognise false information while worrying about others. This could support a discussion prompt: “How should officials communicate with audiences who feel confident judging information but distrust the channels carrying it?” Do not present self-confidence as tested detection ability.
