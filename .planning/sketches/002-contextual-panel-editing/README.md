# 002 — Contextual panel editing

## Design question

How can a builder fluidly inspect and edit charts while independently arranging the dashboard, without authoring chrome changing View-equivalent geometry, losing the selected target, or making either draft scope ambiguous?

## Scope in

- Panel selection without mutation and direct entry to the selected panel's common controls.
- Deliberate common → Advanced disclosure for supported data, content, appearance, axes, interaction, and rare settings.
- A canonical, live representation of the property being changed whenever authoring chrome obscures the original panel.
- Two narrow, independently owned draft scopes may coexist: one dashboard-layout draft for panel order/position, section placement/boundaries, and global layout-preset selection; and at most one selected-chart property draft for that chart's data, content, appearance, axes, interaction, bounded footprint/size, and advanced supported properties. Deterministic packing/reflow is derived canonical output from the combined draft state, not an authored mutation owned by either draft.
- Build-subheader **Save Layout Changes** and **Discard Layout Changes**; Unit Orbit **Save Chart Changes** and **Discard Chart Changes**, including bounded footprint changes; prompt-free Close/Reopen, root Escape, reorder, current-chart resize, and local Preview; visible dirty/suspended scope labels; and retained focus/scroll context.
- Layout-only dirty state never blocks opening, closing, or switching among clean chart inspectors. A chart-target switch asks for resolution only when the current selected chart has dirty property changes, including a dirty footprint.
- Build-to-View departure resolves each dirty scope through its own Save/Discard action or **Stay in Build**. Package replacement, destructive actions, and mutually exclusive authoring workflows remain guarded.
- Loading a preset over meaningful layout work asks **Replace Layout Changes** or **Keep Current Layout**; it never silently overwrites or implicitly saves the layout draft, and any loaded preset preserves every chart's authored footprint while repacking order only.
- Scope-specific validation and deterministic save failure without loss of either draft or partial application.
- A shared Build foundation, independent of the three chart-editor variants: persistent chart-title reordering through chart titles and visible empty grid insertion zones, plus session-local save/load of the current dashboard preset in a parity-safe Build subheader.
- A synthesized sizing direction inside Unit Orbit: one four-track dashboard model and a chart-owned `2×4` footprint picker exposing every discrete `1–4` column by `1–2` row combination, with deterministic derived reflow and controls that stay spatially related to the selected chart without covering it or protected product chrome.
- Hover and keyboard focus preview the candidate rectangle and its explicit `columns × rows` label without changing dashboard geometry. Click, Enter, or Space commits the footprint to the selected-chart property draft; choosing the current footprint is a no-op.
- Invalid out-of-range or stale footprint state—represented by `5×2`—fails selected-chart property validation without mutating the last-good geometry.
- Verified zero-delta View/Build canvas, grid, panel, and plot geometry at `390×844`, `768×1024`, `1024×768`, `1200×900`, and `1440×900`, plus selected-target Unit Orbit clearance at every required authoring viewport.
- Keyboard, touch, non-colour, reduced-motion, long-content, and no-page-horizontal-overflow checks required by the common fixtures.

## Scope out

- Free-form x/y placement, arbitrary panel dimensions, footprints beyond four columns or two rows, or silent coercion of unsupported values. The approved picker remains a bounded set of eight discrete chart-property choices under the Step-10 correction to `SCOPE-04` and the bounded-catalogue rule in `DEFER-12`, not arbitrary resizing.
- Chart creation, Time Group/Scene creation, View Chrono, Present controller, Audience composition, or package-wide authoring.
- Final visual language, icon geometry, theme, typography, spacing, elevation, or production component boundaries.
- Treating the disposable prototype review toolbar as proposed product chrome or product safe area. It is external evaluation scaffolding, remains non-sticky, and may scroll out of view.
- Direct edits to the 12 Aug 2026 UI, temporal, or chart-creation contract sources during Step 4. The user-approved Unit Orbit placement and narrow draft-scope corrections are recorded here for Step 10 codification.
- Quorum protocol/schema changes, production implementation, runtime/state/schema migrations, or a new runtime-only remote dependency.

## Variant hypotheses

### Round one — chart-editor placement

#### A — Unit Orbit

A panel-attached, selected-target-aware command rig orbits the selected target. It places essential, common, and chart-property Save/Discard actions—including bounded footprint/size—at the object, flips or shifts at viewport and protected-product-chrome edges—including lower-row targets—and opens larger chart-local controls only as needed. Global layout-preset controls and layout-draft actions for order/position and sections remain in the shared Build foundation, not inside A. Unit Orbit is the approved Sketch 002 winner.

**Hypothesis:** the shortest target-to-command distance will make repeated local edits fastest and keep scope identity strongest, provided selected-panel and protected-product-chrome clearance remains predictable across all five positions and does not detach suspended-draft actions from the object.

#### B — Tactical HUD

A fixed portal uses the viewport edge opposite the selected target: right portal for left targets, left portal for right targets, and the least-obscuring edge for top, centre/tall, and bottom targets. It is the least-complex React/CSS path of the three. The real dashboard never reflows; if the portal obscures an object-local target, it shows a faithful canonical substitute at the target's canonical View bounds. Direct reordering and presets remain on the shared canvas/subheader; the HUD contains chart-domain controls only. Tactical HUD is preserved as a rejected alternative.

**Hypothesis:** stable controls, explicit opposite-edge placement, and simple implementation geometry will produce the most predictable cross-viewport behavior and lowest cognitive load, even if target-to-control travel is longer than A.

#### C — Focus Bay

Object-local edits enter a full-viewport work bay containing a faithful canonical substitute of the selected panel, the common-to-Advanced editor, and persistent chart-property draft actions. The substitute preserves the canonical panel and plot bounds through uniform scaling rather than internal reflow. Direct reordering and presets remain on the shared canvas/subheader; the Focus Bay contains chart-domain controls only. Focus Bay is preserved as a rejected alternative.

**Hypothesis:** separating high-attention object-local editing from structural grid work will make complex properties and failures easiest to understand, provided the transition remains reversible, preserves focus/scroll, and never disguises a thumbnail or reflowed chart as the canonical substitute.

### Shared Build foundation — not a variant

All charts are directly rearrangeable from their title areas whenever Build is active and the chart editor is closed. Chart titles and visible empty grid insertion zones are peer pointer destinations: a title resolves to that chart's reading-order index, while empty space maps to a discrete row-major insertion index rather than raw x/y coordinates or a persistent hole. On drop, the selected chart retains its authored footprint, every other chart retains its relative order and footprint, and the deterministic four-track packer derives geometry. Alt+Arrow plus first/append keyboard shortcuts expose the same insertion boundaries; dropping at the current boundary is a no-op and does not dirty the dashboard.

One dashboard-layout draft owns panel order/position, section placement/boundaries, and the selected global layout preset. It may coexist with at most one selected-chart property draft, which owns that chart's bounded footprint/size alongside its other chart properties. Deterministic packing derives canonical positions from order, footprints, breakpoint, and container width; reflow caused by a footprint change is output, not layout-draft ownership. This narrow coexistence preserves workflow fluidity because inspecting chart options does not interrupt arrangement work, while independent state ownership keeps every authored mutation, validation problem, Save, and Discard consequence attributable. It does not permit multiple chart drafts.

Global layout-preset load/save, **Save Layout Changes**, **Discard Layout Changes**, local Preview, and the named layout-dirty state live in a constant-height Build subheader; View reserves the same-height product-chrome row so canvas y and grid geometry remain equivalent. Unit Orbit owns **Save Chart Changes**, **Discard Chart Changes**, the chart-owned footprint picker, and the named selected-chart property dirty/suspended state. Local Preview is prompt-free and renders the current combined draft state without committing either scope. Opening, closing, reopening, or switching among clean chart inspectors never resolves or mutates a dirty layout draft. Loading a preset over meaningful layout work requires **Replace Layout Changes** or **Keep Current Layout**. Built-in and session-saved layout presets preserve every chart's authored footprint and repack order only; selector, chart-property ledger, and derived rendered geometry remain coherent without transferring footprint ownership to the layout draft. Shared capabilities persist while comparing candidates and therefore cannot influence the winner.

Build-to-View departure resolves each dirty scope independently before leaving Build. Package replacement, destructive operations that can invalidate either scope, and mutually exclusive authoring workflows—including chart creation, Time Group authoring, and Scene authoring—retain their guarded resolution boundaries.

### Approved draft and prompt matrix

| Event | Required outcome |
|---|---|
| Move or reorder a chart, change section placement/boundary, or choose a global dashboard layout preset | Create or update only the dashboard-layout draft. No prompt. A preset preserves every chart's authored footprint and repacks order only. |
| Commit a valid footprint for the selected chart | Create or update only that selected-chart property draft. No prompt. Hover/focus preview is non-mutating; deterministic packing/reflow is derived output and does not dirty the layout scope. |
| Open, close, reopen, or switch a clean chart inspector while layout is dirty | No prompt. Preserve the layout draft and selected chart context. |
| Change a chart property, including footprint, while layout is dirty | Create or update the one selected-chart property draft alongside the layout draft. Name both dirty scopes. |
| Save or discard chart properties while layout is dirty | Commit or restore only chart properties, including footprint; preserve and revalidate the layout draft. Unit Orbit owns the action and result, and the packer recomputes derived geometry. |
| Save or discard layout while chart properties are dirty | Commit or restore only order/position, sections, and layout-preset selection; preserve and revalidate the chart-property draft, including footprint. The Build subheader owns the action and result. |
| Close, root Escape, reopen, reorder, resize the current selected chart, or use local Preview with either scope dirty | No resolution prompt. Suspend/resume controls or render the combined local draft as appropriate; commit neither scope. |
| Request another chart while current chart properties are dirty | Hold the target request and offer **Save Chart Changes**, **Discard Chart Changes**, and **Stay with Current Chart**. A dirty footprint participates in this chart-property prompt; layout state does not. |
| Load a preset over meaningful layout work | Offer **Replace Layout Changes** and **Keep Current Layout**. Do not silently replace, save, or discard layout work; either outcome preserves chart footprints. |
| Request View with either scope dirty | Keep View pending and resolve each dirty scope independently through its scoped Save/Discard action or **Stay in Build**. |
| Package replacement, destructive operation, or chart creation/Time Group/Scene authoring | Guard according to the state the action can invalidate; mutually exclusive authoring never creates another concurrent mutation draft. |
| Validation or save failure in one scope | Retain both drafts and last-good dashboard; associate the error and Retry action only with the attempted scope. |

### Round two — universal footprint synthesis within Unit Orbit

**Size** starts and remains in Unit Orbit beside the selected chart. The orbit follows or repositions around the selected target at top, centre, side, and lower-row positions and may never overlap that selected actual panel or protected product chrome. Protected product chrome includes, at minimum, the shared Build subheader and its global controls. Unrelated dashboard panels may be covered and do not participate in orbit placement or collision calculations. This product-chrome exception preserves reachability and does not revive displaced-panel or affected-neighbour logic. A z-index-only overlap is invalid because whichever surface is visually higher still obscures the other.

The disposable review toolbar is external evaluation scaffolding, not product chrome: it remains non-sticky, sits outside product placement calculations, and consumes no product safe area. When clearance around the selected panel and protected product chrome leaves an unusably small region—less than 280 px in this prototype—Unit Orbit may invoke the already-approved ephemeral canvas recenter/scroll behavior. That movement changes only viewport position, never canonical grid tracks, order, panel x/y/w/h, or plot geometry. The orbit then settles clear of the selected panel and protected chrome and scrolls its own long controls internally. The explicit prototype tradeoff is that review controls may scroll out of view; returning to page top restores them.

The global Build subheader continues to own only dashboard-wide order/position, section and layout-preset controls plus layout-draft Save/Discard; Unit Orbit owns bounded footprint/size and all chart-property Save/Discard. The selected chart and its current authored footprint remain understandable throughout prompt-free picker preview, scoped save, and scoped discard; deterministic reflow is the packer's derived canonical output, not an authored layout mutation.

#### Approved sizing synthesis — universal `2×4` footprint picker

One four-track dashboard model exposes eight chart-owned, discrete footprint choices for every chart: `1×1`, `2×1`, `3×1`, `4×1`, `1×2`, `2×2`, `3×2`, and `4×2`. The picker is a two-row by four-column grid. A cell's column and row distance from the top-left origin defines the candidate footprint; the rectangular area from that origin through the cell is visibly filled, and an explicit text label announces the result as `n columns × n rows`.

Hover and keyboard focus are preview-only: they update the filled picker rectangle and size label but never reflow or dirty the dashboard. Pointer click, Enter, or Space commits the previewed footprint to the selected-chart property draft. Applying the already-selected cell is a no-op. A deterministic, order-preserving four-track packer then recomputes derived chart geometry with uniform horizontal and vertical gaps; that reflow does not create or mutate a layout draft, and the chart retains its committed footprint when reordered.

Built-in and session-saved dashboard presets must preserve the selected picker cell, accessible size label, and selected-chart property ledger while repacking order only; derived rendered geometry must stay in agreement. A stale or out-of-range `5×2` footprint exercises selected-chart property validation and recovery while preserving the last-good geometry. At the narrowest verified viewport, the authored four-track dashboard remains intact inside an internally pannable shell rather than silently rewriting the saved footprint.

#### S1 — Curated footprints — superseded, preserved

S1 offered only Narrow `1×1`, Standard `2×1`, Wide `4×1`, and Tall `2×2`, with chart-type-specific subsets. It is superseded because it unnecessarily withholds valid `3×1`, `1×2`, `3×2`, and `4×2` combinations, makes builders translate semantic names back into geometry, and creates chart-specific cardinality states that the universal picker no longer needs. Its useful four-track simplicity is retained in the synthesis.

#### S2 — Adaptive lattice — superseded, preserved

S2 offered a twelve-track grid with quarter, third, half, two-thirds, and full-width spans. It is superseded because the finer lattice adds fractional choices and implementation complexity without improving the directness of a visible `2×4` footprint target. It also makes uniform spatial rhythm and outcome prediction harder than the bounded four-track model. Its useful principle—more flexibility than S1—survives through all eight combinations.

#### S3 — Composed rows — rejected, preserved

The dashboard is assembled from row-owned arrangements such as Solo, Pair, Trio, and Feature plus supporting charts. A chart is reordered into a row position, but the row template owns its widths and height; changing one chart's requested emphasis therefore changes the composition rather than a chart-owned size.

**Rejected after structural review:** the current row-owned model excludes charts taller than one row and has no coherent control location. Putting those row controls inside Unit Orbit would visually and behaviorally imply that the selected chart owns the change even though the composition owns the resulting geometry. S3 remains preserved as evidence, but is no longer a live sizing candidate.

## Winner or synthesis

**Approved — A: Unit Orbit with the universal chart-owned `2×4` footprint picker.** Direct chart-title/empty-space reordering and order-only dashboard presets remain the shared Build foundation rather than a competing editor variant. Tactical HUD, Focus Bay, S1, S2, and S3 remain interactive or documented as preserved rejected evidence.

## Why it won

Unit Orbit provides the strongest spatial and transactional association between the selected chart and its controls. The footprint picker lives where chart properties are inspected, previewed, saved, and discarded; its visible rectangle communicates both dimensions, exposes all eight bounded choices without arbitrary dragging, and distinguishes consequence-free hover/focus exploration from deliberate click/keyboard commitment. The four-track packer retains predictable rhythm and implementation economy while deterministic reflow remains derived output rather than layout-owned state. Responsive exercise confirmed that the selected chart stays clear, the Orbit stays usable, and View/Build geometry remains equivalent.

## Why each alternative was rejected

- **S3 — Composed rows: rejected and preserved.** Its row-owned model currently excludes charts taller than one row and offers no coherent control location. Putting it inside Unit Orbit would falsely signal chart ownership even though the composition owns the change. Its composition rhythm may inform a future global layout preset, but it does not remain a chart-sizing candidate.
- **S1 — Curated footprints: superseded and preserved.** It omitted four valid footprints, required builders to decode names into geometry, and introduced chart-specific zero/one/many choice branches. The synthesis retains its four-track simplicity without those restrictions.
- **S2 — Adaptive lattice: superseded and preserved.** Its fractional twelve-track lattice introduced more choice and packing complexity than the task needs, with weaker predictability and rhythm. The synthesis retains its flexibility goal through eight bounded, visibly mapped footprints.
- **B — Tactical HUD: rejected and preserved.** It detaches the same chart controls from the selected object, increases pointer and attention travel, and its fixed edge provides no compensating benefit for repeated chart-local inspection and resizing. Its simple portal mechanics remain useful implementation input.
- **C — Focus Bay: rejected and preserved.** Its full-viewport transition interrupts the dashboard-context loop and introduces a substitute/context switch for common edits. Its faithful canonical-substitute rule remains valuable when a selected object genuinely cannot remain visible.

## Retained cherry-picks

- A retained direction: direct target-command association, selected-panel and protected-product-chrome clearance, and edge flipping.
